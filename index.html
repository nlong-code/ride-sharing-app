Ride-Sharing App

Week 3 – High-Level Design Diagrams | Group 3

System Design Overview

This document builds on the Week 1 stakeholder analysis and the Week 2 requirements for the Ride-Sharing App. It includes a high-level architecture diagram, an application-level data flow diagram, and a Level 1 data flow diagram.

The design reflects the needs of passengers, drivers, and administrators, with attention to upfront fare transparency, trip tracking, secure payments, reliability, accessibility, and data privacy.

External users and services Application services Application processes Data storage

1. High-Level Architecture Diagram

Shows the major parts of the application and how they connect.

How it works: Passenger, driver, and administrator interfaces connect to the backend. The backend coordinates accounts, rides, fare calculations, tracking, payments, support, and notifications. The application also communicates with external mapping/GPS and payment services.

2. Data Flow Diagram – Application Overview

Shows the information exchanged between the app and its external entities.

How it works: Passengers send account details, ride requests, locations, and payment information to the app. Drivers send availability and trip updates. The app exchanges route information with the mapping service and payment requests with the payment processor.

3. Data Flow Diagram – Level 1

Breaks the application into five main processes and shows the related data stores.

How it works: The Level 1 diagram separates the main functions into account management, ride requests and fare calculation, driver matching and trip tracking, payment processing, and ratings/support. It also identifies user, ride/trip, and payment records used by the system.

Design Notes

  • The passenger should see the fare breakdown before confirming a ride.
  • The agreed fare stays fixed unless the passenger changes the destination or adds a stop and approves the revised price.
  • Drivers can accept or decline requests and provide trip status and location updates.
  • Payment failures should be communicated to passengers, with an option to retry or contact support.
  • Security, privacy, accessibility, reliability, and performance should guide the design.

These diagrams are initial Week 3 design artifacts. The group can revise them after reviewing the requirements and receiving feedback.
